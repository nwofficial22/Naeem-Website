🌡️ Featured Data Analytics Project

Atlanta Weather and Heat Trends

Analyzed historical NOAA weather data to evaluate temperature and extreme-heat trends in Atlanta. Used Excel PivotTables, data filtering, and charts to examine average maximum temperatures in April and the number of days exceeding 95°F during June, July, and August. The analysis showed an upward trend in April temperatures, while recent years (2023–2025) experienced levels of extreme-heat days comparable to some of the higher periods in the dataset.

🔍 Key Finding

The results suggest that Atlanta is experiencing signs of increasing heat, highlighting the importance of preparing communities for periods of extreme temperatures.

💡 Recommendation

Proposed mobile cooling centers that could be deployed to neighborhoods experiencing high heat exposure. This approach could improve access to cooling resources for residents without reliable transportation while allowing services to be relocated based on where extreme heat impacts are greatest.

📋 Survey Analytics & Modeling

Food Ecosystem Analysis Survey

Cleaned and analyzed survey data from 354 respondents using advanced Excel formulas and weighted scoring models to evaluate food-access barriers and classify participants into five distinct resource tiers. The study examined socioeconomic indicators, transportation challenges, and food behaviors to inform municipal and community food policy.

⚙️ Weighted Scoring Model (70/30)

• Access Score (70% Weight): Averaged 5 factors (income, education, employment, transportation, distance to primary food source), each rated on a 1–5 scale. Weighted at 70% due to its decisive influence on overall access.
• Food Behavior Score (30% Weight): Averaged 4 behavioral metrics (grocery shopping frequency, transportation challenges, cooking, and gardening participation) on a 1–5 scale.
• Tier Classification: Combined weighted scores placed each respondent into one of five tiers, ranging from Tier 1 (High Resource) to Tier 5 (High Food Access Barriers).

📊 Key Insights & Findings

• Resource Distribution: The majority of respondents possess medium to good food access. The largest group falls into Tier 2 (Upper Middle), followed by Tier 3 (Stable Working), indicating stable baseline access with occasional financial or accessibility friction.
• Multifaceted Determinants: Revealed that food access extends beyond grocery store proximity—transportation availability, financial resources, education, and cooking proficiency all play essential roles.

🎯 Decision Impact & Technical Execution

• Actionable Policy: Provides empirical data to help city leaders and community organizations make targeted decisions regarding food access investments, transit expansion, and equitable food systems.
• Data Cleaning: Converted qualitative survey responses (such as Agree/Disagree and text entries) into numerical values for analysis.
• Excel Techniques: Utilized complex nested IF statements, weighted averages, and lookup functions to calculate respondent tier rankings.
